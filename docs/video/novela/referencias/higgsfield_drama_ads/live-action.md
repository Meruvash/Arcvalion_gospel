# Live action (`live action`)

Photoreal scenes shot like a feature film; the default look. Family: live action.

## Style line

Cinematic live action, camera-ready faces in soft, bright, flattering light from the scene's own sources, natural colour.

## Style block

None. The sheets (`sheet-realism.md`, `sheet-stylized.md`) and the location sheets (`location.md`, THE MASTER BLOCK) carry this
look in their own fixed words.

## Light words

None; `location.md` (THE LIGHT) writes each place's light.

## Image words

None.

## Video words

"Shot like a feature film: close shots at eye level with shallow depth of field, the speaking face sharp and the room behind it
soft; camera-ready faces, real skin under everyday makeup; every face fully lit and easy to read in soft, bright light from the
scene's own source, the shadows open, a catchlight in the eyes; natural colour, skin warm, whites clean; in a single the face
turns three-quarter, the eyes to one side of the lens; the camera locked off or pushing in slowly, moving only with someone who
walks; the actors play small and real, their eyes on each other. Clean, close, quiet voices over the room's own sound; the place
makes its own sounds as they happen. No music and no score. No subtitles or on-screen text."

## Acting

- **Small:** held looks, a slow blink, a breath before the line, lips pressed together, a glance down and back up, a slight head
  tilt, a smile that stays in the mouth.
- **Eyeline:** to the scene partner, just beside the lens. In a single the face turns three-quarter toward the partner's side, the
  eyes to that side of the lens: closes written without it came out centred and square-on. Only a narrator's or testimonial
  closing line goes straight to the lens, in a medium close (the references end on one).
- **The listener acts too:** the listener's face gets its own shot during the other's line.
- **Busy hands:** hands stay busy with the scene's own props while people talk.
- **Pauses:** lines are quiet and conversational, with 1 to 3 seconds of silence between them, written into the beat timing.
- **Faces:** camera-ready, written into each human's appearance and wardrobe facts so the sheet carries them: hair styled for the
  part, groomed brows, everyday makeup, clothes well cut and fitting. Our faces came out plain beside the references' groomed
  casts.
- **Age reads true:** lines, grey hair, the neck and hands of the age; older characters attractive at their age, never smoothed
  younger.

## Motion and physics

Real weight and grip: liquids tilt and settle, fabrics catch the light as the body turns, hair and coats follow the head and the
stride, fingers really close around what they hold.

## Light and camera

- **Coverage:**
  - Each scene opens on a wide or medium-wide that shows the room's depth and who stands where, then cuts in.
  - Dialogue goes in singles: over the listener's shoulder (their out-of-focus shoulder and the back of their head at one side
    edge), or clean, the speaker alone with the face turned three-quarter toward the listener's side. In a vertical frame the
    speaker's head and shoulders fill its upper two-thirds. Reverse on the next line.
  - Close inserts of hands and props go between lines.
  - Two people walking and talking are shot from the front, knees up, the camera moving back ahead of them at their pace.
- **Rhythm:** a new shot on each line or reaction, 3 to 5 seconds each, written as "Cut to …" beats; a walk-and-talk or a two-shot
  runs 6 to 10 seconds. The references hold their shots a median of 2.7 to 4.8 seconds, longer than the 2 to 4 we cut at.
- **Camera:** locked off or a slow push-in on close shots, a slow sideways arc around a character at work; no shake, no fast
  moves.
- **Light:** soft, bright and flattering, from the scene's own sources: every face fully lit and readable, shadows open. Written
  as a low sun raking across the room, the far side of the face in shadow and the colour muted, all three runs of one test came
  out low-key, the faces half dark and the colour flat, beside bright, flattering references.
  - By day: warm, creamy light through the windows, the faces on the window side bright, the room around them light. A low, raking
    sun only for a golden-hour beat.
  - At dusk: the cool blue sky in front, warm candles or a lit doorway behind, the faces lit warm and readable.
  - At night indoors: table lamps and a ceiling fixture fill the room with soft warm light, the faces readable, the windows
    blue-black.
  - At night outdoors: downlights from a canopy or porch, the lit interior warm behind.
  - Under shop or office fluorescents: skin stays warm and whites neutral (ours turned teal-green there).
  - Faces modelled softly: the key side bright, the far side a step darker and still readable, catchlights. Colour natural, skin
    warm, one or two rich accent colours in the wardrobe; never muted or low in saturation.

## Pitfalls

- **Faces into the lens:** a dialogue close written plainly ("close shot of the speaker") comes out frontal and centred, eyes into
  the lens. Write every dialogue close over the listener's shoulder, or as a clean single with the face turned three-quarter
  toward <the listener's side>, the eyes to that side of the lens.
- **Flat face light:** with a lamp in the room, a night clip lit both faces evenly from the front, grey-teal and low in contrast.
